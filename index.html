What I offer

Tools built around how your business works, not a generic app you have to work around.

  • Your paper forms, as an app. Time sheets, daily reports and job checklists, turned into a simple app your crew fills in on their phone. Hours bill right, and the week doesn’t get pieced together from memory.
  • Your tools talking to each other. I connect the software you already use, so information is entered once and goes where it needs to.
  • The repeat work, done on its own. Invoices, reminders, follow-ups and client updates that go out by themselves, so clients know where things stand without calling to ask.
  • The week at a glance. One place to see hours, jobs and what’s still outstanding, so the day is planned and nothing slips.
  • Straight advice on AI. Where it would actually save you time, and where it wouldn’t. I’ve built AI features into my own app and cut them when they didn’t hold up, so you’ll get a straight answer.

Second Pass

secondpass.app

An iPhone app I designed and built. I’d been scrolling reels for far too long when a video came up saying, more or less, go do something with your life. It didn’t say anything groundbreaking, and that’s when I realized we already have this knowledge. We just need to be reminded of it. So I built an app that reminds you in your own words: you talk into it, and later it plays them back to you. It’s in testing with its first users.

It’s where I learned to cut what doesn’t work, even after it’s built.

About me

I’ve worked in the trades for ten years, operating heavy equipment and dealing with clients every day. When I have a clear objective for the day, it reflects in the work and in how the whole project goes. Everything runs smoother, and everybody works together better.

I want to build tools that give a team that kind of clarity: they handle the day-to-day of running a business without adding to the noise. I think keeping a clear mind is essential to a good work environment.

A calmer team, and clients who know what’s going on.

Book a free walk-through

I come by, or we talk on a call if you’re not nearby. I’ll ask where things get lost: with your clients, with your team, between the job and the office. Then I’ll ask to see how it’s done now, whether that’s a paper form, a group chat or a spreadsheet.

Within a few days you get one to three options in writing, each with what it would cost and how long it would take. No obligation. And if it isn’t worth building, I’ll tell you that too.

If you go ahead, the first step is a clickable prototype, a small paid job, so you can see how it will work, and try it, before anything gets built.
